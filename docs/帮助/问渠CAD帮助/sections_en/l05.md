### What you will be able to do {#l05-goal}

<ul>
<li>Tangent constraints: line to circle, line to arc; answer “Yes” to the “Sketcher constraint substitution” question.</li>
<li>Mirror (the Symmetry tool): draw one side, generate the other.</li>
<li>Trim: click away unwanted pieces one by one; know that Trim cuts at the nearest intersections.</li>
<li>Expressions: type <code>=</code> in a dimension to refer to other values; spreadsheets: one sheet controls a set of dimensions.</li>
</ul>

This lesson makes the **crank plate outline**: a circle of diameter {{cr.D}} on the main shaft, a circle of diameter {{cr.d}} for the crank pin, {{cr.e}} apart, joined by two tangent lines into one loop and padded {{cr.t}} mm. (The real WQ-LS crank also has a sector-shaped counterweight below; lesson 7 adds it.)

### Mirror and trim {#l05-trim}

Two circles and two tangent lines cross at several points. Trim removes the piece you click **between the nearest intersections on either side** — so the large circle needs three clicks on top (first the middle, then the two small pieces up to the tangent points) and the small circle one click at the bottom. Try it here first:

::video{src=wqcad-l05a-en title="Recording: two circles, tangents, mirror, trim, tangents again (about 90 s)"}

Two details in the recording:
- The second Tangent pops up “Sketcher constraint substitution”: FreeCAD offers to replace “point on circle + tangent” with a single “endpoint tangency”. Choose Yes.
- The mirrored line is geometry only, without constraints; after the trims join its ends to the arcs, add two tangents just as on the right.

### Expressions and spreadsheets {#l05-sheet}

Type <code>=</code> first in a dimension box and it becomes an expression: arithmetic (<code>=33/2</code>) or a reference to another object's value (<code>=Spreadsheet.B1</code>). Keep a set of key dimensions in a spreadsheet: change a number in the sheet and every dimension and part that refers to it updates — that is how part families are made and how calculation results are carried into the model.

::video{src=wqcad-l05b-en title="Recording: centre distance in a spreadsheet, the sketch refers to it, pad; change the sheet and the crank follows (about 85 s)"}

In the recording the plate volume is {{cr.v1}} mm³ with a centre distance of 15.5; with 18 in the sheet it becomes {{cr.v2}} mm³ after recompute.

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <code>=Spreadsheet.B1</code> | The value in cell B1 |
| <code>=Spreadsheet.e</code> | By name, after giving B1 the alias e (right-click the cell → Properties → Alias) |
| <code>=Sketch.Constraints.D / 2</code> | Half of the sketch dimension named D |
| <code>=Pad.Length + 2</code> | The pad length plus 2 |

### Follow along {#l05-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>New Parametric Body, sketch on XY: a circle of diameter 33 centred on the origin and one of diameter 22 on the Y axis.</li>
<li>On the right draw a line with its ends on the two circles and add two tangents (answer Yes).</li>
<li>Select the line, click Symmetry, then click the Y axis: the left side appears.</li>
<li>Trim: three pieces on top of the large circle, one at the bottom of the small one. Add two tangents to the left line.</li>
<li>Spreadsheet B1 = 15.5; in the sketch add a vertical distance from the origin to the small circle's centre: <code>=Spreadsheet.B1</code>; pad 10.</li>
<li>Change B1 to 18, recompute, watch the crank; change it back to 15.5.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l05-self}

1. Give B1 the alias <code>e</code> and change the expression to <code>=Spreadsheet.e</code>.
2. Put the two diameters in B2 and B3 and make the diameter constraints read them too.
3. Mirror again with “Create symmetric constraints” ticked: what does FreeCAD report after trimming, and how do you deal with it? (Redundant constraints, lesson 4.)

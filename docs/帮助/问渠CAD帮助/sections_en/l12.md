### What you will be able to do {#l12-goal}

<ul>
<li>Import an STL mesh and make it semi-transparent to trace over.</li>
<li>The difference between a polyline, an interpolating B-spline (through every point) and a control-point B-spline (pulled by the points).</li>
<li>Lock a few points of an unconstrained trace before adding dimensions, so it doesn't drift.</li>
<li>Extrude to a surface (Create solid unticked); Section of two surfaces = their intersection curve.</li>
</ul>

### Why reverse-engineer {#l12-why}

The machine's head exists only as a triangle mesh (`head.stl`, 36 000 triangles): you can see it, assemble it and get its volume, but you can't change dimensions, add fillets or make machining drawings. Reverse engineering rebuilds real surfaces and solids from a mesh (or a 3D-scan point cloud). This module does only the outer shape of the head's upper box (z ≥ 120); the access windows and internal ribs are left out.

### Side outline: polyline + interpolating B-spline {#l12-side}

::video{src=wqcad-l12a-en title="Recording: import head.stl → semi-transparent → XZ sketch: polyline + interpolating B-spline for the front-top corner (about 65 s)"}

| B-spline | How it is drawn | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Interpolating (through points) | every clicked point lies on the curve | tracing scan points and meshes |
| Control points | the curve doesn't pass through the inner points; they pull it | designing your own fair shapes (continuity, lesson 13) |

Compared with the mesh outline (the final version after lesson 13; 60 sample points per segment measured to the mesh outline), the side outline deviates at most {{l12.dev_side}} mm, {{l12.dev_side_mean}} mm on average — the maximum is where the left line moved about 1 mm when tangency was added in lesson 13; the front outline at most {{l12.dev_front}} mm. Cast blanks are typically toleranced around ±1 mm, so this is good enough.

### Front outline: lock, fillet, radius {#l12-front}

::video{src=wqcad-l12b-en title="Recording: YZ sketch: polyline round four corners → lock two corner points → Fillet → radius 14 (about 60 s)"}

- The sketch Fillet tool: click the point where two lines meet and it rounds the corner, keeping the old corner as a construction point (“Preserve corner”).
- Locking a point adds a horizontal and a vertical distance from the origin. It only pins the traced position; it doesn't move it.

### 3D curves: intersecting two surfaces {#l12-section}

::video{src=wqcad-l12c-en title="Recording: extrude both outlines into surfaces → select both → Section → show only the curves (about 60 s)"}

The side outline extruded along Y and the front outline along X intersect in the 3D edges of the head's shape. The front-top corner is a B-spline seen from the side and an arc seen from the front; together they make a 3D curve that can't be drawn in any single sketch.

Two other ways to get 3D curves (described, not recorded):
- **Projection**: Part workbench Projection on surface, projecting a sketch curve onto a surface along a direction (e.g. a nameplate outline on the housing).
- **Helix**: the Part Helix primitive with pitch, height and radius — the basis of lesson 8's spring.

### Follow along {#l12-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>New file, File → Import `head.stl`; set the mesh's transparency to 70.</li>
<li>Sketcher, new sketch, XZ: polyline (−55, 227.6) → (−55, 120) → (75, 120) → (75, 248) → (−28.5, 243.4); interpolating B-spline from (−28.5, 243.4) along the mesh to (−55, 227.6).</li>
<li>New sketch, YZ: polyline round (−48, 120), (70, 120), (70, 248), (−48, 248); lock the bottom-left and top-right points; fillet the two top corners, radius 14.</li>
<li>Part workbench: extrude both sketches (side: forward 48, reverse 70; front: forward 75, reverse 55), Create solid unticked.</li>
<li>Select both extrusions, Section.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l12-self}

1. Trace the front-top corner with a control-point B-spline instead: where do the control points have to go for the curve to follow the mesh?
2. Trace the narrower lower part of the head (z from 40 to 120) and join it to the upper part.
3. Project a 30 × 10 rounded rectangle (a nameplate) onto the side surface.

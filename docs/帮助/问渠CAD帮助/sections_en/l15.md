### What you will be able to do {#l15-goal}

<ul>
<li>An assembly holds links to parts; the parts stay in their own files, and changing a part updates the assembly.</li>
<li>Every assembly needs at least one grounded part; the others are positioned by joints.</li>
<li>A joint = one element (circular edge, face, point) on each of two parts; FreeCAD aligns them and leaves only the motion that joint allows.</li>
<li>Degrees of freedom: a free part has 6; a revolute joint leaves 1 (rotation), a slider 1 (translation).</li>
</ul>

### The needle-bar mechanism is a slider-crank {#l15-mech}

The upper shaft turns the crank; the crank pin drives the rod; the rod's small end drives the needle-bar link; the needle bar slides up and down in its bushes. This lesson uses 4 parts (each file is in its machine-STEP position):

| Part | Contains | In the mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | front bearing + needle-bar bushes (fixed in the head) | grounded |
| ShaftCrank | upper shaft (cut at x = 60) + crank + crank pin | rotates (crank radius 15.5) |
| Rod | connecting rod | swings (55 between holes) |
| NeedleBarAssy | needle bar + needle-bar link | slides up and down |

Shaft, crank and crank pin are screwed together in the machine, so here they are one part, saving a few fixed joints.

### Insert and ground {#l15-insert}

::video{src=wqcad-l15a-en title="Recording: Create assembly → Insert component: Frame (grounded), ShaftCrank, Rod, NeedleBarAssy (about 60 s)"}

- Before inserting parts from other files, save the assembly file (and the part files must be saved).
- For the first part FreeCAD asks whether to ground it — Yes.
- Later parts are offset a little so they don't overlap; their position doesn't matter, the joints pull them into place.

### Add joints {#l15-joints}

::video{src=wqcad-l15b-en title="Recording: activate the assembly → Revolute ×3 (shaft/bearing, crank pin/rod big end, link pin/rod small end) → Slider (needle bar/bushes) (about 85 s)"}

| Joint | Pick | Motion left |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed | any two elements | none |
| Revolute | two circular edges (coaxial) | rotation about the axis |
| Cylindrical | two circular edges | rotation + translation along the axis |
| Slider | two circular edges or faces | translation along the axis |
| Ball | two points | rotation about the point |
| Distance, parallel, perpendicular, angle | faces, edges | constrain one quantity |

Counting: 3 moving parts × 6 = 18; three revolutes and a slider remove 5 each, 20 in all — more than 18 because in a planar mechanism some constraints repeat (redundancy), which the solver handles. In effect only the crank rotation is left: 1 DOF; the crank angle fixes where the rod and needle bar are.

### Follow along {#l15-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>Open the 4 part files; create a new file and save it.</li>
<li>Assembly workbench: Create assembly, Insert component: Frame (ground: Yes), ShaftCrank, Rod, NeedleBarAssy.</li>
<li>Revolute: the frame's bearing-bore edge at x = 51 + the shaft's edge at the same place.</li>
<li>Revolute: crank-pin end edge + rod big-end bore edge; rod small-end bore + link pin.</li>
<li>Slider: the bush bore edge + any circular edge of the needle bar.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l15-self}

1. Replace the slider by a cylindrical joint and drag the needle bar: now it can rotate — in reality the needle clamp fixes its orientation, hence the slider.
2. Don't merge parts: insert shaft, crank and crank pin separately and join them with two fixed joints.
3. Drag the crank (with the assembly active, drag a part) and watch the rod and needle bar follow.

### What you will be able to do {#l10-goal}

<ul>
<li>Datum planes, lines and points: helper geometry that isn't part of the solid, used as references for sketches and features.</li>
<li>Attachment: pick references (plane, face, edge, point), an attachment mode, then an attachment offset (move, rotate).</li>
<li>Why datums: they don't depend on a face number of the solid, so the model doesn't “lose a face” when it changes.</li>
<li>Parametric design: expressions in dimensions, referring to a spreadsheet; put intent like “the pin is at mid-height” into the model.</li>
</ul>

### Datums and attachment {#l10-datum}

| Datum | Common attachment modes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Datum plane | Plane (on a plane, then offset); three points; normal to edge; tangent to surface | a sketch on a side, an inclined mounting face |
| Datum line (axis) | two points; along an edge; intersection of two planes | axis of an inclined hole, a revolve axis |
| Datum point | vertex; edge midpoint; circle centre | hole position, measuring origin |
| Local coordinate system | any | tilting a group of features |

The attachment offset is in the **reference's own coordinates**: attached to the YZ plane, “Z” is the YZ plane's normal, i.e. global X.

### Needle-bar link: a pin sketched on a datum plane {#l10-pin}

The WQ-LS needle-bar link: a 12 × 14 × 16 block with a Ø8 vertical bore for the needle bar, and a Ø6 pin 17 long out of one side (to the take-up link). The block is a lesson-6 pad, made before the recording.

::video{src=wqcad-l10a-en title="Recording: datum plane on YZ, offset 6 → Ø6 circle at height 8 on the datum → Pad 17 (about 75 s)"}

The volume is {{l10.pin}} mm³. The link in the machine STEP is {{l10.real}} mm³ — it also has two set-screw holes in the front (On your own).

### Parametric: change one number, everything follows {#l10-param}

“The pin is at mid-height” is design intent. Write it as expressions: pad length = <code>Spreadsheet.B1</code>, pin height = <code>Spreadsheet.B1 / 2</code>.

::video{src=wqcad-l10b-en title="Recording: spreadsheet height 16 → expressions for pad length and pin height → change to 22: taller block, pin still in the middle (about 75 s)"}

At 22 the pin height becomes 11 by itself and the volume is {{l10.v22}} mm³. A good parametric model:
- keeps the key dimensions in one sheet, with meaningful names (aliases);
- writes relationships between dimensions as expressions (half, equal, sum) instead of typing the same number in several places;
- has fully constrained sketches, and features that refer to datum planes and axes rather than “face number n”.

### Follow along {#l10-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>New body, on XY: a 12 × 14 rectangle centred on the origin plus a Ø8 circle, Pad 16.</li>
<li>New datum plane: reference the YZ plane, mode Plane, attachment offset Z = 6.</li>
<li>Select the datum plane, new sketch: Ø6 circle, centre on the vertical axis, vertical distance 8. Pad 17.</li>
<li>Spreadsheet B1 = 16; pad length <code>=Spreadsheet.B1</code>; the pin sketch's vertical distance <code>=Spreadsheet.B1 / 2</code>.</li>
<li>Change B1 to 22 and recompute: taller block, pin in the middle.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l10-self}

1. Add two set-screw holes in the front (Y = −7) at heights 4 and 12, Ø3.75, down to the bore (use a datum plane or click the front face). The volume should come close to {{l10.real}} mm³ (at height 16).
2. Give B1 the alias <code>h</code> and change both expressions to <code>Spreadsheet.h</code>.
3. Add a row “width” = 12 and make the datum offset = width / 2: change the width and the pin stays on the side face.

### What you will be able to do {#l06-goal}

<ul>
<li>Tell the four basic features apart: Pad (extrude, add), Pocket (extrude, remove), Revolution (around an axis, add), Groove (around an axis, remove).</li>
<li>For a turned part, sketch only <b>half the section</b>, with one side on the axis.</li>
<li>Small sketches: zoom in to draw, then lock the position with Horizontal / Vertical distance.</li>
<li>Hole tool: place a point in a sketch first; choose thread type, size, depth and bottom shape in the panel.</li>
</ul>

### The four basic features {#l06-four}

| Feature | What it does | Sketch needs | Example in this lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pad | extrude along the normal, add material | closed profile | crank pin Ø10 × 25 |
| Pocket | extrude along the normal, remove material | closed profile, on a face or plane of the solid | (on your own) |
| Revolution | revolve around an axis, add material | closed half section, must not cross the axis | needle bar Ø8 × 180 |
| Groove | revolve around an axis, remove material | same | oil groove on the needle bar |
| Hole | cut a hole to a standard | points or circles in a sketch | M3 threaded hole in the pin end |

Which one: the cross-section is the same all along (plate, pin, block) → Pad; the part is symmetric about an axis (shaft, rod, sleeve, wheel) → Revolution.

### Needle bar: Revolution and Groove {#l06-bar}

The WQ-LS needle bar is 8 in diameter and 180 long, with two oil grooves 0.5 wide and 0.2 deep at 39.25 and 41.25 mm from the bottom, and a 0.5 chamfer at each end. This lesson makes the rod and the first groove:

::video{src=wqcad-l06a-en title="Recording: half-section rectangle → Revolution → zoom in, small rectangle, lock its position → Groove (about 85 s)"}

- After the first corner of a rectangle, just type “width, Tab, height, Enter” and the sizes become constraints; the first corner on the origin becomes coincident — fully constrained in one go.
- The Revolution axis defaults to the vertical sketch axis; with the half section drawn against it, the result is a solid rod.
- The groove rectangle is only 0.2 × 0.5: zoom in to over a hundred pixels per millimetre to draw it, then lock its lower-left corner with Horizontal distance 3.8 and Vertical distance 39.25.

Volumes: plain rod {{l6.v0}} mm³; one groove {{l6.v1}}; two grooves {{l6.v2}}. After the 0.5 chamfers in lesson 7 it is {{l6.v3}} — the same as the real needle bar in the machine, {{nb.v}} mm³.

### Crank pin: Pad and Hole {#l06-pin}

::video{src=wqcad-l06b-en title="Recording: circle Ø10 → Pad 25 → sketch on the end face with one point → Hole: M3, tap drill, depth 7.5, flat bottom (about 85 s)"}

| Hole panel item | Choice here | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard (thread type) | ISO metric regular profile | also fine thread, UTS (inch), pipe threads |
| Size | M3x0.5 | the diameter fills in automatically |
| Hole type | Tap drill (to be threaded) | Clearance: screw clearance hole (3.4 for M3); Tap drill: tap-drill size (2.5 for M3), no thread geometry; Modeled thread: real thread geometry (slow — only for 3D-printed threads) |
| Depth | 7.5 mm | or Through all |
| Drill point angle | unticked | ticked gives a 118° drill point; unticked is flat |
| Head type | None | counterbore and countersink are here |

The result is {{l6.pin}} mm³. In the machine STEP this hole is drawn as a plain Ø2.95 hole (simplified), so the real part is {{l6.pin_real}} mm³ — the difference is just the hole diameter. Not modelling thread geometry is normal practice: the model stays light and drawings label “M3” automatically.

### Follow along {#l06-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>New body, sketch on XZ: rectangle from the origin, 4 wide, 180 high. Close the sketch, Revolution 360°.</li>
<li>Another sketch on XZ: zoom in at the right edge, draw a 0.2 × 0.5 rectangle, lower-left corner at horizontal distance 3.8, vertical distance 39.25. Groove 360°.</li>
<li>Second groove: the same, vertical distance 41.25. The volume should be {{l6.v2}} mm³. Save as “NeedleBar” for lesson 7.</li>
<li>New file: circle Ø10 on XY, Pad 25.</li>
<li>Click the top face, new sketch, one point on the origin; close the sketch.</li>
<li>Select that sketch, click Hole: ISO metric regular, M3x0.5, tap drill, depth 7.5, drill point unticked.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l06-self}

1. With Pocket, cut a slot 1 wide and 1.5 deep across the other end of the crank pin (not on the real part — practice): sketch on the end face, a rectangle across it, Pocket 1.5.
2. Change the hole type to Clearance and see the diameter; then add a counterbore and see which parameters appear.
3. Can both needle-bar grooves be drawn in <b>one</b> sketch and cut with a single Groove? Try it.

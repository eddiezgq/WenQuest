### What you will be able to do {#l13-goal}

<ul>
<li>Trim two (or more) surfaces against each other: let each enclose a solid, then take their Common.</li>
<li>Recognise continuity: G0 just touches (a crease), G1 also matches direction (tangent, no edge), G2 also matches curvature (smoothest reflections).</li>
<li>A crease often starts in a sketch: two segments meet at an angle. Add Tangent and it becomes G1.</li>
<li>Two faces that are each smooth can meet in a sharp edge: blend it with a fillet or a filling.</li>
</ul>

### Continuity: G0, G1, G2 {#l13-g}

| Level | Meaning | Looks like | How to get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| G0 position | joined, no gap | a crease; highlights break here | segments only share an endpoint |
| G1 tangent | same direction too | no edge, but highlights “kink” at the seam | endpoint tangency; fillets |
| G2 curvature | same bending too | highlights flow straight through | Surface workbench Filling/Blend with curvature; higher-degree B-splines |

Most mechanical parts are fine at G1 (cast and machined fillets are G1); appearance parts, handles and car bodies care about G2.

After Common the upper head is {{l13.common}} mm³. ### G0 → G1: tangency in the outline {#l13-tangent}

::video{src=wqcad-l13b-en title="Recording: edit the side outline → delete the coincident at the B-spline ends → endpoint tangency at both ends → the left crease is gone (about 65 s)"}

- An interpolating B-spline only passes through the clicked points; its end direction is whatever the fit gives, usually not the neighbouring line's.
- Endpoint tangency already includes coincidence: delete the old coincident constraint first, or it's redundant and the sketch won't solve.
- With tangency, the B-spline shifts a little (its end direction is straightened) and the left line moves slightly too — in reverse engineering, smoothness and fit to the mesh are a trade-off.

The angle between the faces at the left edge: {{l13.before}}° before tangency, 0° after.

### Blend: a fillet on the sharp edges {#l13-blend}

::video{src=wqcad-l13c-en title="Recording: select the two sharp edges at the ends of the front-top corner → Part Fillet R8 (about 50 s)"}

The fillet is tangent to both sides (G1). The result is {{l13.fillet}} mm³. For G2: Surface workbench Filling (Surface_Filling), with these edges as the boundary and continuity set to curvature — it doesn't cut a fillet from the solid, it builds a new face from boundaries, which is then sewn to the others (lesson 14).

The creases along the sides of the top (sloping top vs R14 rounds) are left as they are. Done properly, the R14 round wouldn't be a straight cylinder but a round swept along the sloping top edge — see On your own.

### Follow along {#l13-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>Open lesson 12's file and delete the Section; set Solid to true on both extrusions.</li>
<li>Select both extrusions, Common. Look round for the three kinds of crease.</li>
<li>Double-click the side outline: in the constraint list delete the coincident at each B-spline end; B-spline endpoint + line endpoint, Tangent; same at the other end. Close the sketch.</li>
<li>Select the two sharp edges at the ends of the front-top corner, Part Fillet, radius 8.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l13-self}

1. The creases along the top: replace the front outline's R14 rounds by a round swept along the sloping top edge (lesson 8's pipe), intersect again and see whether the crease remains.
2. Surface workbench: cut away part of the front-top corner and fill it with Filling, trying position, tangent and curvature continuity; compare the highlights.
3. If you have the Curves workbench add-on, turn on zebra stripes and compare G1 and G2.

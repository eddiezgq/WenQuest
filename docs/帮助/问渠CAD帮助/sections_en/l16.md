### What you will be able to do {#l16-goal}

<ul>
<li>Simulation: write a motion formula for a revolute (or slider) joint, run kinematics, play.</li>
<li>Scrub the time slider to any instant and measure the stroke.</li>
<li>WenQuest MBD: give the parts materials, compute drive torque and joint forces at the real speed; read the checks before the results.</li>
<li>Measure: distances and angles between points, edges, faces.</li>
<li>Interference: whether two parts overlap.</li>
<li>Exploded views: pull parts apart along directions to show the assembly and make assembly drawings.</li>
</ul>

### Making the mechanism move {#l16-sim}

::video{src=wqcad-l16a-en title="Recording: Create simulation → add motion: shaft revolute, 2*pi*time → Run kinematics → play → time slider to 0 s and 0.5 s (about 65 s)"}

- Angles are in radians: `2*pi*time` is one turn per second; with start 0, end 1 s and step 0.01 s there are 101 frames.
- Formulas can also give constant acceleration, sinusoidal swings, steps (Help in the motion dialog shows examples).
- Needle-bar stroke: a script read the needle bar's position in all 101 frames; highest minus lowest is about 31 mm (frames don't land exactly on the dead centres; the recordings read 30.94–31.00) = 2 × the crank radius 15.5. The needle-bar axis passes through the crank axis (no offset), so the stroke is exactly twice the crank radius.

### How much force: WenQuest MBD {#l16-mbd}

::video{src=wqcad-l16b-en title="Recording: AI Parts → assembly multibody dynamics → parts have no material → View → Material, Steel-1C45 for all four parts → read the assembly → upper-shaft revolute, constant 5000 rpm → kinematic, 0.012 s in 120 steps → Compute → checks, drive torque, crank-pin force → Play (about 150 s)"}

- FreeCAD's own simulation only shows how things move. WenQuest MBD (AI Parts workbench, menu “AI Parts → 装配体多体动力学”, assembly multibody dynamics) reads the assembly's parts and joints and works out the forces.
- Mass and inertia come from the solids and the material density. With no material it stops and says what is missing; it does not guess. All four parts here are C45 steel: crank 0.202 kg, rod 0.058 kg (the machine's parts list gives 58.2 g for the needle-bar rod), needle-bar group 0.088 kg.
- Kinematic analysis: the motion is given (5000 rpm, constant), and it computes the drive torque and the joint reactions at every instant. One turn at 5000 rpm takes 0.012 s.
- Checks first: constraint residual 4e-15 (limit 1e-8) and zero degrees of freedom both pass, so the results can be trusted. It also reports removing redundant constraints (the slider and the revolute joints over-constrain the needle bar); reaction components in those directions are not unique and are marked incomplete.
- Results: peak drive torque about 4.82 N·m, close to the 4.70 N·m for the needle mechanism in the machine's design calculation G2 (that model has more parts, so the numbers differ a little). Peak crank-pin force (Joint001) about 753 N — lesson 19 uses it for the crank stress.
- “Write report…” makes a Word report (conclusions, model, checks, curves), plus a PDF on computers with LibreOffice.

### Measure, interference, exploded views {#l16-check}

| Task | How |
|---|---|
| Distances, angles | toolbar Measure: click two points, edges or faces; the result shows in the view and can stay in the model |
| Interference | select two parts, Part workbench Common: zero volume means no interference; otherwise that's the overlap. Check at several instants (different slider positions) |
| Exploded view | Assembly workbench Create view: select parts and drag them apart along a direction, in several steps; then a TechDraw assembly drawing (lesson 18) |
| Bill of materials | Assembly workbench Create bill of materials: parts and quantities, exported as a table (lesson 18) |

### Follow along {#l16-do}

<ol class="steps">
<li>Open lesson 15's assembly and double-click Assembly to activate it.</li>
<li>Create simulation, add a motion: the shaft's revolute joint, formula <code>2*pi*time</code>.</li>
<li>Run kinematics, play; set the time slider to 0 s and 0.5 s and look at the dead centres.</li>
<li>Measure the needle-bar link's height difference between the two instants: 31 mm.</li>
<li>Switch to the AI Parts workbench and open assembly multibody dynamics; give all four parts the material Steel-1C45; read the assembly; upper-shaft revolute at constant 5000, kinematic, duration 0.012, 120 output points, Compute.</li>
<li>Once the checks pass, look at the drive-torque and crank-pin reaction curves and note the peaks.</li>
</ol>

### On your own {#l16-self}

1. Change the formula to `pi/2*sin(2*pi*time)`: the crank swings ±90°. How does the needle bar move?
2. Imagine the rod's hole spacing changed to 45: does the stroke change? Does the lowest point? (Hint: stroke depends only on the crank radius.)
3. At the 0.25 s frame, check with Common whether the rod and crank interfere.
4. Compute again at 2500 rpm: what is the peak drive torque now? (Inertia forces go with the square of speed, so it should be close to a quarter.)

Everyone has heard the word "robot", yet it is not easy to say clearly what a robot is, what parts it is made of and what it can do. In everyday language, robot arms in factories, vacuum cleaners at home and chat programs on a phone are all called robots; engineering, however, needs a concept clear enough to discuss design, standards and safety. This chapter is the introduction to the whole book, and it answers exactly these most basic questions: what a robot is, where it came from, what kinds there are, what parts it consists of, where it is used, and how this book will explain it step by step.

The chapter is arranged as follows:

- Section 1.1 starts from the definition in the international standard ISO 8373, uses the "sense–decide–act loop" to show that what fundamentally distinguishes a robot from ordinary automated equipment is autonomy, and discusses the boundaries of the definition;
- Section 1.2 reviews the history along four threads, automata and programs, feedback and control, industrial robots, and intelligence and learning, and uses the statistics of the IFR to show the scale of the field today;
- Section 1.3 classifies robots along two threads: by application, and by structure and mode of locomotion;
- Section 1.4 takes a robot apart into seven parts, mechanism, actuation, electrics, sensing, control, software and intelligence, and introduces the concept of degrees of freedom that runs through the whole book;
- Section 1.5 presents applications in manufacturing, logistics, medicine and the home, and uses real data from the WenQuest digital factory to compute handling, bottlenecks and payloads;
- Section 1.6 gives the road map of the book's 61 chapters and introduces the five robots that run through the whole book: the UR5e, the Panda, the Go2, a differential-drive AGV and the X2 quadrotor.

This chapter requires only high-school mathematics: functions, trigonometric functions and simple plane geometry. Every computed result in the book is produced by a program, which readers can open to inspect and modify. The concepts of this chapter come up again and again in later chapters: degrees of freedom are treated rigorously in Chapter 11; the sense–decide–act loop is the starting point of Part V on control; and from Chapter 12 on, the parameters of the five robots run through the parts on kinematics, dynamics, control and programming.

After studying this chapter, the reader should be able to:

1. state the definitions of robot, industrial robot, service robot and medical robot given in ISO 8373, and use the sense–decide–act loop to decide whether a device is a robot and on which boundary of the definition it lies;
2. describe the main milestones of robot development along the four threads, and use the compound-growth formula to compute a compound annual growth rate from statistical figures;
3. classify a robot by application, structure, mode of locomotion and other dimensions, and compare the workspaces of different structures;
4. explain the role of each of the seven parts of a robot, compute the degrees of freedom of a rigid body and of an open-chain mechanism, and decide whether a robot is underactuated;
5. for a specific task (such as machine tending and material handling in the digital factory), estimate the handling time, the bottleneck capacity and the payload required;
6. give the main parameters of the five robots that run through the book, and choose a reading path through the book according to their own goals.

---

This chapter did only one thing: find the pose of the end-effector from the joint variables. All of its methods can be condensed into one formula,

$$
T(\theta) = e^{[\mathcal S_1]\theta_1}\, e^{[\mathcal S_2]\theta_2} \cdots e^{[\mathcal S_n]\theta_n}\, M ,
$$

and the one sentence behind it: **Every joint is a screw axis, the motion of a joint is a motion about that axis, and the motion of the whole arm is these motions composed in order.**

The formula has only two inputs: the home pose M, and the screw axes of the joints in the home configuration. The screw axis of a revolute joint, $\mathcal S = (\hat\omega, -\hat\omega \times q)$, is given by the direction of the axis and any point on it, and does not depend on the choice of the point; the screw axis of a prismatic joint, $\mathcal S = (0, v)$, contains only the direction of sliding. The motion of a joint is the matrix exponential $e^{[\mathcal S]\theta}$; for a revolute joint it is a turn about an axis that need not pass through the origin.

The key to the derivation is to imagine "turning from the last joint backwards": each time a joint turns, the links between it and the base have not yet moved, so its axis is always where it was in the home configuration. Moving M to the left of the exponentials, and replacing each screw axis by its expression in the end-effector frame, $\mathcal B_i = [\mathrm{Ad}_{M^{-1}}]\mathcal S_i$, gives the body form. The two forms give the same result; which to choose depends on where the data come from and what will be changed later: when the tool is changed, the space form changes only M; when the base is changed, the body form changes only M.

On the three typical structures, the UR5e, the Panda and a SCARA, the formula is written in exactly the same way, and the check against the 3D models of the parts library agrees to the level of rounding error. Compared with the manufacturer's DH parameters, the model differs by one or two millimetres. This reminds us that forward kinematics, however precisely computed, can only be as accurate as its parameters; high-precision work needs calibration.

The order of the product of exponentials cannot be reversed. If it is reversed, the turns are made about axes that are no longer there, and the result may land outside the workspace. There are two correct readings: from the outside in, the axes stay fixed in the home configuration; from the inside out, the outer axes are updated each time a joint turns. The "current axes" of the latter are exactly the columns of the Jacobian matrix in Chapter 16.

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| Forward kinematics | Finding the pose of the end-effector from the joint variables |
| Home configuration | The configuration when all joint variables are zero; it must agree with the convention of the controller |
| Screw axis | Six numbers describing a joint axis: for a revolute joint the first three are the direction of the axis and the last three are computed from a point on the axis, independent of the choice of point; for a prismatic joint the first three are 0 and the last three are the direction of sliding |
| Space form and body form | The screw axes are expressed in the base frame or in the end-effector frame of the home configuration respectively; the results are the same |
| Adjoint matrix | The 6×6 matrix that changes a screw from one frame to another |
| Redundancy | More joints than the degrees of freedom of the end-effector; the same end-effector pose has infinitely many sets of joint angles |
| Absolute accuracy | The ability to reach "the computed position", limited by errors in the parameters; different from repeatability |

### Historical notes

| Year | Who | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1763 | Mozzi | Pointed out that every instantaneous motion of a rigid body is a screw motion about an axis |
| 1830 | Chasles | Proved that every rigid-body displacement is a screw motion about an axis (Chapter 6) |
| 1876, 1900 | Ball | *The Theory of Screws* (first edition 1876, enlarged in 1900 as *A Treatise on the Theory of Screws*) set out the geometry and mechanics of screws systematically |
| 1955 | Denavit, Hartenberg | Proposed the DH method, describing adjacent joints by four parameters (Chapter 13) |
| 1983 | Hayati | Pointed out that DH parameters are sensitive to errors when adjacent axes are parallel, and proposed modified parameters |
| 1984 | Brockett | Proposed the product of exponentials formula for the kinematics of robot arms |
| 1994 | Murray, Li, Sastry | *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation* taught robot kinematics systematically with the product of exponentials as its backbone |
| 2017 | Lynch, Park | *Modern Robotics* made screws and the product of exponentials the backbone of the whole book |

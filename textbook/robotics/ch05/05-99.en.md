title: Chapter Summary
---

The main thread of this chapter can be summed up in one sentence: **the position and orientation of one frame relative to another are written as a 4×4 homogeneous transformation matrix; every conversion between frames is a product and inversion of such matrices, in the order fixed by the subscript cancellation rule.**

We started from two frames with different origins. A position vector depends on the origin, so converting the coordinates of a point needs, besides the rotation, the translation between the two origins: $p_a = R_{ab}\,p_b + p_{ab}$. This is an affine transformation, not a linear one; free vectors such as velocities, forces and directions transform by the rotation matrix alone. All the information needed to describe one frame relative to another is the orientation $R_{ab}$ and the origin position $p_{ab}$, together called the pose, with 6 independent parameters.

Giving a point a fourth component 1 and a direction a fourth component 0 turns the affine transformation into a linear transformation in four dimensions. The first three columns of the homogeneous transformation matrix $T_{ab}$ are the three axes of {b}, and the fourth column is the origin of {b}; to read a matrix is to read off a frame. All homogeneous transformation matrices form the special Euclidean group SE(3), which describes exactly the rigid-body transformations, those that preserve distances and right-handedness. The same matrix has three uses: describing a pose, converting coordinates, and moving a body as a displacement operator.

Composition and inversion obey the subscript cancellation rule: $T_{ab}T_{bc} = T_{ac}$, $T_{ab}^{-1} = T_{ba}$. The $R^{\mathsf T}$ in the inversion formula $T^{-1} = (R^{\mathsf T},\ -R^{\mathsf T}p)$ must not be left out: it transfers the reversed arrow to the other set of axes to be read. A premultiplied displacement is relative to the fixed frame, a postmultiplied one relative to the body itself; unlike in SO(3), a premultiplied rotation in SE(3) turns about the origin of the fixed frame and swings the tool far away.

The frames of a workstation are organized into a frame tree: each frame records only its pose relative to its parent, and the pose between any two frames is the product along the unique path between them in the tree, with an upward step multiplying by an inverse. A robot model file is itself a chain of frames, and multiplying along the chain is forward kinematics. The tree stores no loops; loops serve as constraints for checking and calibration.

Finally, the camera–arm–workpiece grasping problem comes down to a closure equation: the path through the robot and the path through the camera must meet at the grasp point. The subscript cancellation rule then gives the target pose of the flange. The error analysis shows that distance magnifies angular errors: a small angular error δ causes a position miss of about δ times the length of the lever; shortening the chain of frames and calibrating the key poses directly are the fundamental ways of improving accuracy.

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| Coordinate frame | An origin plus three mutually perpendicular unit axes forming a right-handed system |
| Pose | The orientation and origin position of one frame relative to another; 6 independent parameters |
| Affine transformation | A linear transformation plus a constant translation; converting the coordinates of a point is of this kind |
| Homogeneous coordinates | A point gets a final 1, a direction a final 0 |
| Homogeneous transformation matrix | The first three columns are the axes, the fourth column is the origin |
| SE(3) | The group formed by all homogeneous transformation matrices, that is, all rigid-body transformations |
| Rigid-body transformation | A mapping that preserves distances and right-handedness |
| Subscript cancellation rule | Adjacent equal subscripts cancel; the first and last subscripts that remain give the meaning of the product |
| Frame tree | Each frame records only its pose relative to its parent; the path between any two frames is unique |
| Lowest common ancestor | The first frame at which the two upward chains meet |
| Closure equation | When two paths reach the same frame, their poses must be equal |
| Eye-to-hand / eye-in-hand | The camera is fixed in the workstation / mounted on the robot's wrist |

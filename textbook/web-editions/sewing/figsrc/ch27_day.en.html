Without shared data

1Options live in spreadsheets and chat messages; "micro-oil hook" is copied as a standard hook
2MRP does not know DLC coating is a 5-day subcontract; the schedule uses the standard hook lead time
3Control boxes arrive, but nobody knows which firmware version to flash
4The HMC stops for 3 h; paper job tickets reveal it after the shift, and the bottleneck output is short
5Hook clearance is written on paper; it creeps up and nobody plots the trend
6Test results sit on the test-bench PC, filed by date, not matched to serial numbers
7Three months later a customer reports skipped stitches; nobody can tell which hook lot is in the machine

How the digital factory closes the gap (Sections 27.3–27.7)

The configurator issues variant code WQ-LS-TED; options become the BOM
The variant BOM carries the subcontract step and its lead time; MRP includes it
Firmware is a BOM line with a version; the test bench checks it against the work order
Machine states go on the bus; the dashboard shows stoppages and the bottleneck live
Measurements go on the bus; control chart updates live, rules raise alarms
Test results go to the historian by serial number and stay with the machine record
Serial-to-lot links trace it in minutes and fence the scope

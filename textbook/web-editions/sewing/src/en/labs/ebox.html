Sewing Machine Design and Manufacturing, Chapter 18 · Virtual lab 18-1 · Faster switching means less heat but more interference; longer burn-in means fewer returns but higher cost

Current and temperature over two sewing cycles (grey: phase current; red: junction temperature; blue: heat sink)

Switching-time trade-off (other parameters as currently set; green region: both limits met; dots: current setting)

Model (illustrative, the same as the book’s model18.py): DC link 310 V; switch on-state drop 1.0 V + 0.1 Ω, diode 0.9 V + 0.08 Ω, modulation index × power factor 0.6; switching loss per device = ½ × 310 V × (Ipk/π) × tsw × fsw, diode reverse recovery taken as 30% of that; rectifier bridge 2 × 0.9 V × 0.6 I. Each start–stop uses the start–stop current for 0.15 s of acceleration and 0.15 s of deceleration, the sewing current for the rest of the sewing time, and zero otherwise. Junction to heat sink per device 3.5 K/W, time constant 0.05 s; heat-sink thermal resistance × (1 + 1.5 × clogging); temperature rise inside the box 0.6 K/W × (1 + clogging) × (average power-stage loss + 8 W other). Capacitor core 5 °C above the box interior, life by the ten-degree rule, rated at 105 °C; 4800 h powered per year. Peak common-mode current = 300 pF × 310 V /(tsw/2).

Effect of burn-in time on first-year return rate (blue) and total cost per unit (orange) at the current burn-in temperature

Model (illustrative, the same as the book’s model18.py): a fraction p of units has early defects, whose failure times follow a Weibull distribution (shape 0.5, scale 100 h, under use conditions); random failure rate of sound units 1×10⁻⁶ /h; the first year is taken as 4800 h. Acceleration factor from the Arrhenius relation, activation energy 0.7 eV, components at 55 °C in use and 15 °C above the burn-in chamber during burn-in; components must not exceed 85 °C, so the chamber is limited to 70 °C. Cost = burn-in CNY 0.3 per unit·h + return CNY 600 per unit × return rate + CNY 50 for handling each unit that fails during burn-in.

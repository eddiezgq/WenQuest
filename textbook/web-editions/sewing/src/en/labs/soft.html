Cantilever droop: shape under its own weight of a strip of material overhanging the clamp by L (solid: current material; light grey: four common fabrics)

Model (same as the book’s model23.py): bending rigidity by the FAST formula B (μN·m) = 9.8×10⁻⁶ × mass per area (g/m²) × bending length³ (mm), i.e. B = w c³ (w = weight per unit area); the material is treated as a horizontally clamped large-deflection cantilever under its own weight (the elastica under gravity), and its shape is found by energy minimisation. The chord angle is the angle between the horizontal and the line from the clamp to the free end; at an overhang L = 2c it is about 41.5° (the definition of bending length in the cantilever test). Pushing force: a strip 5 cm long in the pushing direction (fixed, independent of the overhang above) and b wide, clamped at one end and pushed forward, with the other end free to move sideways and self-weight neglected, buckles at F = π²Bb/(4L²) (the support case most prone to buckling, so a lower bound). The shape depends only on L/c: mass per area changes only the value of the rigidity, not the drooped shape. Fabric data are worked-example values.

Unattended completion rate of the whole workflow = per-step success rate to the power n (curves: several per-step success rates; dot: current setting)

Payback period vs per-step success rate (other parameters as currently set)

Model (same as the book’s model23.py): each garment piece goes through n actions (separating, picking up, smoothing, aligning, loading…), each with success rate p; on failure a person must intervene for t_fix seconds, then the step is redone. Expected failures per piece = n(1−p)/p; cycle time = n × t_step / p + failures × t_fix. Assuming one robot replaces one operator per shift (with comparable output), annual labour saved = shifts × wage × 8 h × 300 days, minus the labour for handling failures and annual maintenance (10% of the price). Wage and price are worked-example values.

Annual electricity use of the whole factory: clutch motors vs servo (direct drive)

Model (same as the book’s model23.py): annual consumption = number of machines × powered-on hours × average power. The default average powers are taken from a published retrofit case (20 lockstitch machines converted from clutch motors to servo motors): clutch 0.45 kW, servo 0.25 kW, i.e. about 45% saving; the emission factor 0.57 kg CO₂/kWh and electricity price 0.7 yuan/kWh are worked-example values. A clutch motor idles whenever it is powered, whereas a servo only delivers power while sewing, so the longer the powered-on time and the smaller the share of actual sewing time, the larger the saving; the default values do not distinguish the two.

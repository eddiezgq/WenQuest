    Feed zone, side view (looking along the edge, feed left to right)

    Blue is the main feed dog (behind the needle; sets the stitch length); orange is the differential feed dog (in front of the needle). The small ticks on the fabric start 2 mm apart. When the differential dog travels farther than the main dog, the fabric between them is pushed together and the ticks crowd; when it travels less, the fabric is stretched and the ticks spread out.

    Finished seam edge (top view)

    ε > 0: the seam is longer than the fabric; when released, the extra edge length buckles into waves (the familiar “fluted edge” on knits). ε < 0: the fabric is longer than the seam and is gathered into fine pleats.

    Edge strain vs differential ratio

    The green band is the flat zone |ε| ≤ 2%; the dot is the current setting. The curve levels off at both ends because once pushing or stretching exceeds what the fabric can take, the dogs start to slip.

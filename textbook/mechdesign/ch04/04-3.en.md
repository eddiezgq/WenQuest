### 4.3.1 Three kinds of fit

The relation between the tolerance zones of a hole and a shaft of the same nominal size that are assembled together is called a **fit**. The size of the hole minus the size of the shaft is a clearance when positive and an interference when negative. Fits fall into three kinds according to the relative position of the hole and shaft tolerance zones:

- **Clearance fit**: the tolerance zone of the hole lies entirely above that of the shaft, and every pair has a clearance (the minimum clearance may be zero). The shaft can turn or slide in the hole.
- **Interference fit**: the tolerance zone of the hole lies entirely below that of the shaft, and every pair has an interference. Assembly requires pressing, or heating the hole or cooling the shaft, and forces and torques are transmitted by the interference.
- **Transition fit**: the two tolerance zones overlap, and a pair may have either a clearance or an interference. It is used where good centering is needed and the parts must still be dismountable.

The limits of clearance (or interference) are calculated directly from the limit deviations of the two zones:

$$X_{\max} = ES - ei, \qquad X_{\min} = EI - es$$

A negative result is an interference: $Y_{\max} = -X_{\min}$, $Y_{\min} = -X_{\max}$. The difference between the two is called the fit tolerance, and it equals the sum of the hole and shaft tolerances: $T_f = X_{\max} - X_{\min} = T_h + T_s$. The accuracy of a fit is determined by the hole and the shaft together (this is the inequality of Section 4.1).

### 4.3.2 Hole-basis and shaft-basis systems

The same fit can be obtained from different combinations of hole and shaft. To reduce the number of sizes of tools and gauges, the standard provides two systems of fits:

- **Hole-basis system**: the fundamental deviation of the hole is fixed at H (EI = 0), and the various fits are obtained by changing the fundamental deviation of the shaft. Holes are machined with fixed-size reamers and broaches and inspected with plug gauges; fewer hole tolerance zones mean fewer such tools. The hole-basis system is therefore generally preferred.
- **Shaft-basis system**: the shaft is fixed at h (es = 0), and the hole is changed. It is used where several parts with different fit requirements are mounted on one plain shaft (such as a piston pin that fits both the piston and the connecting rod), or where the shaft is cold-drawn bar whose outer surface is not machined further.

A rolling bearing is a standard part, and the system of fits is set by the bearing: **the inner ring and the shaft follow the hole-basis system, the outer ring and the housing bore the shaft-basis system.** But the tolerance zone of the bearing inner ring is special: it lies below the zero line (upper deviation 0, GB/T 307.1 / ISO 492), whereas an ordinary H hole lies above the zero line. The same k6 shaft therefore gives a transition fit with an H7 hole but an interference fit with a bearing inner ring. On drawings only the tolerance class of the shaft (or housing bore) is given at a bearing, such as Ø35k6 or Ø72H7, not a fraction (Section 3.5).

### 4.3.4 Choosing fits

To choose a fit, first decide the system (generally hole-basis), then choose the fundamental deviation and tolerance grade of the shaft according to the function. Common choices (the preferred fits recommended by GB/T 1800.1 and the experience collected in handbooks) are:

- **Running and sliding**: H7/f7, H8/f7 (ordinary running, such as plain bearings); H7/g6 (precision sliding, such as the guide bushes of a robot linear module; for still higher requirements, the selective assembly of Section 4.1); H7/h6 (location, can be pushed in by hand).
- **Location, dismountable**: H7/js6, H7/k6 (good centering, assembled and dismantled with a mallet, such as couplings and gears that transmit torque through keys); H7/n6 (tighter, assembled with a press).
- **Transmitting load, not dismantled**: H7/p6, H7/r6, H7/s6 (assembled by pressing or by temperature difference; the force is transmitted by the interference, or by interference and key together).
- Tolerance grades: up to IT8, the hole is one grade coarser than the shaft (such as H7/k6), because a hole is harder to machine and to measure than a shaft; for coarser grades the hole and shaft take the same grade.

The fits of rolling bearings are determined by the direction of the load relative to the ring (GB/T 275): **a ring that rotates relative to the direction of the load (rotating load) needs a tight fit; a ring that is stationary relative to it (stationary load) may have a loose fit.** SH-301 turns with the gear while the radial force keeps a fixed direction, so the inner ring rotates and carries a rotating load and takes the k6 interference; the outer ring does not rotate and carries a stationary load and takes the H7 clearance. During assembly the outer ring can then be pushed into place by the end cover and shims, and in service it can move within the axial clearance provided as the shaft expands thermally (Section 4.8). The heavier the load, and where there are shocks, the tighter the fit (m6, n6).

### 4.3.5 The effect of temperature on fits: a collaborative-robot joint

**Example 4.3.2** The two 6010 bearings of the collaborative-robot shoulder joint RJ-201 (Section 33.10) are mounted in the joint housing. The housing is aluminum alloy and the bearing outer rings are bearing steel, and their coefficients of linear expansion differ by a factor of two. The motor and the harmonic reducer generate heat, and in service the housing and the outer rings are both about {{ex4_3.dT:.0f}} K warmer than at assembly (20 °C) (this book's estimate). For the same temperature rise, the bore of the aluminum housing expands more than the steel outer ring by

$$\Delta = (\alpha_{\text{Al}} - \alpha_{\text{steel}})\, D\, \Delta T = (23 - 11.5)\times 10^{-6} \times {{ex4_3.D_o:.0f}} \times {{ex4_3.dT:.0f}} = {{ex4_3.loosen:.0f}}\ \mu\text{m}$$

A fit whose clearance or interference amounts to only ten or a few tens of micrometers is shifted by {{ex4_3.loosen:.0f}} μm by temperature alone. Program 4.3.1 compares three housing bores (outer ring 0/{{ex4_3.out10_lo:.0f}} μm):

| Housing bore | Cold | At working temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Ø80 H7 (+{{ex4_3.H7_80_hi:.0f}}/0) | Clearance 0–{{ex4_3.H7_cold_max:.0f}} | Clearance {{ex4_3.H7_warm_min:.0f}}–{{ex4_3.H7_warm_max:.0f}} |
| Ø80 K6 ({{ex4_3.K6_hi:.0f}}/{{ex4_3.K6_lo:.0f}}) | Interference {{ex4_3.K6_cold_Ymax:.0f}} to clearance {{ex4_3.K6_cold_Xmax:.0f}} | Clearance {{ex4_3.K6_warm_min:.0f}}–{{ex4_3.K6_warm_max:.0f}} |
| Ø80 M6 ({{ex4_3.M6_hi:.0f}}/{{ex4_3.M6_lo:.0f}}) | Interference {{ex4_3.M6_cold_Ymax:.0f}} to clearance {{ex4_3.M6_cold_Xmax:.0f}} | Interference {{ex4_3.M6_warm_Y:.0f}} to clearance {{ex4_3.M6_warm_max:.0f}} |

With H7, as commonly used in steel housings, the clearance in the aluminum housing reaches {{ex4_3.H7_warm_max:.0f}} μm when warm. The outer ring may then creep in the bore and wear it, and the stiffness and positioning accuracy of the joint vary with temperature. The bearing bores of aluminum-alloy housings should therefore be chosen tighter (K6, M6), or a steel sleeve pressed into the bore. A tighter fit has its own price: the maximum cold interference is {{ex4_3.M6_cold_Ymax:.0f}} μm (M6), which squeezes the outer ring and reduces the radial internal clearance of the bearing, so that if necessary a bearing with the next larger clearance group (C3) is chosen; and the floating end, which must slide axially, should not be too tight either. Robot joints undergo large temperature changes, and their fits must always be checked at the working temperature. On the inner-ring side, both the shaft and the inner ring are steel and temperature has little effect; the 6010 inner ring on Ø50 k5 has an interference of {{ex4_3.k5_50_Ymin:.0f}}–{{ex4_3.k5_50_Ymax:.0f}} μm.

**Engineer's notes**

- A fit is written on drawings as a fraction (hole above, shaft below); at a bearing only the tolerance class of the part that mates with the bearing is written.
- For parts with an interference fit, state the method of assembly (pressing, heating, cooling) and the pressing force or heating temperature; once the interference has been calculated, its strength is checked in Chapter 25.
- Where aluminum-alloy housings, plastic gears and steel shafts are used together, calculate the fits once at the highest and once at the lowest working temperature.

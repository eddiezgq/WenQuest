### 4.5.1 Datum features and datums

Orientation, location and run-out tolerances all need an answer to "relative to what?". On drawings a filled (or open) triangle is placed on a face or a cylindrical segment of the part and connected to a letter in a square, showing that it is a **datum feature** (Figure 4.5.1). The real surfaces of a part always have form errors and cannot serve directly as datums. A **datum** is an ideal feature established from the datum feature. A real plane is laid on a precision surface plate, and the surface of the plate is the datum plane; a real journal is laid in two precision V-blocks of equal height or held in a contracting collet (a hole is held on an expanding mandrel), and the axis they determine is the datum axis. The surface plate, the V-blocks and the collet are called the datum feature simulators; in inspection they stand for the datum.

**The datum is chosen on the surface by which the part is located when it is fitted in the machine.** This is the first rule for choosing datums. With the right datums, the geometrical tolerances on the drawing control the relations that really matter for the part in the machine.

### 4.5.2 A common datum: A–B of SH-301

SH-301 is supported in the housing by two bearings, and the axis about which it rotates is the common axis of the two bearing seats. The datum of SH-301 is therefore the common datum A–B established jointly by the two bearing seats: the two bearing seats are labeled A and B, and "A–B" is written in the frame (Figure 4.5.1a). For inspection, both bearing seats are laid at once in two V-blocks of equal height (or on a pair of precision rollers), the shaft is turned about their common axis, and the radial circular run-out of the gear seat, the seal seat and the overhang is measured.

In manufacture, SH-301 is ground between the center holes at its two ends; the center holes are the process datum and A–B is the design datum. If the two bearing seats and the other diameters are all ground in the same setup, the error between the two datums is very small (the "unity of datums" of Section 3.3). If the drawing took the easy way and wrote "relative to the axis of the center holes", inspection would be more convenient, but the center holes play no part in the machine, and the drawing would not be controlling the function.

### 4.5.3 The three-plane datum system: the pillow block

A rigid body has 6 degrees of freedom in space. To "settle" a part on an inspection table, three mutually perpendicular datum planes are needed, forming a **three-plane datum system**:

- **Primary datum** A: the part rests on it with at least 3 points of contact, removing 3 degrees of freedom (one translation and two rotations);
- **Secondary datum** B: perpendicular to A, with at least 2 points of contact, removing 2 more;
- **Tertiary datum** C: perpendicular to both A and B, with 1 point of contact, removing the last one.

This is "3–2–1" location (Figure 4.5.1b). The pillow block of the palletizing conveyor is mounted on a cross-member of the frame: its base sits on the cross-member (primary datum A), one side rests against a locating lip of the cross-member (secondary datum B), and its position along the cross-member is set by the anchor bolts (the tertiary datum, which may be a side face C or the anchor-bolt holes themselves). The position of the bearing bore, the center height and the position of the anchor-bolt holes are all toleranced relative to this system. **The order of the datums cannot be changed at will**: with A first and B second, the part is first laid flat on A and then pushed against B. If the order is changed, the part is set down differently, and the same part gives different measurements. The datum letters in the frame, from left to right, give this order.

Fixture design follows the same reasoning: the workpiece is located in the fixture by 6 locating points that remove its 6 degrees of freedom (Chapter 51 of *Manufacturing Technology*). When the design, process and inspection datums coincide, the error is smallest.

**Engineer's notes**

- Prefer as datums large, machined surfaces that locate the part in assembly; small surfaces or as-cast surfaces make unstable datums for measurement.
- A common datum formed by two or more features (A–B) does not mean the same as two datums (A and B written in two compartments): in the former the two have equal standing, in the latter one comes before the other.
- A coordinate measuring machine first "sets up the coordinate system", that is, it simulates the datums from measured points in the order of the datums on the drawing. If this is done wrong, every result that follows is wrong.

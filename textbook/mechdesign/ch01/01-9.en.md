### 1.9.1 Structure of the book

The book consists of an introduction and nine parts, {{fig1_9.n_ch}} chapters in all (the colored band at the top of Figure 1.9.1):

- **Introduction.** Chapter 1: what machine design is and how engineers work.
- **Part I Design Methods and Engineering Communication.** Chapters 2–6: the design process, engineering drawings and 3D modeling, tolerances, materials, manufacturing processes.
- **Part II Strength, Stiffness and Failure.** Chapters 7–13: loads, stress, deformation, static strength, fatigue, friction and wear, finite elements.
- **Part III Analysis and Design of Mechanisms.** Chapters 14–23: structure and kinematics of mechanisms, linkages, cams, gear mechanisms, gear trains, other common mechanisms and compliant mechanisms, force analysis, balancing, multibody dynamics.
- **Part IV Joints and Elastic Elements.** Chapters 24–27: threaded fasteners, keys and interference fits, welding and adhesive bonding, springs.
- **Part V Mechanical Drives.** Chapters 28–32: belts, chains, gears, worm gears, precision reducers, drive systems.
- **Part VI Shafts, Supports and Moving Components.** Chapters 33–37: shafts, rolling bearings, plain bearings, linear motion components, couplings and clutches.
- **Part VII Modern Design Methods and Digitization.** Chapters 38–44: vibration, optimization, reliability, precision design, design of experiments, CNC machining, the digital thread.
- **Part VIII Mechanical Design of Robots and Intelligent Equipment.** Chapters 45–49: joint modules, robot arms, mobile and legged robots, end effectors, automation equipment.
- **Part IX Integrated Design.** Chapters 50–52: the reducer design project, the collaborative-robot joint module, the automated workstation.

### 1.9.3 The two products used throughout the book

As far as possible the examples in the book are based on two products, with consistent parameters throughout:

1. **The WQR-105 two-stage cylindrical gear reducer**, a product of the WenQuest digital factory (parts library [[part:C-RED-WQR105]]). This chapter starts from its bill of materials and gear ratios; later chapters use it as the example for materials, strength, gears, shafts, bearings, keys, bolts, lubrication, tolerances, machining and inspection; and the design project of Chapter 50 designs it completely.
2. **A collaborative-robot joint module**: servo motor, harmonic drive ([[part:D-RDC-HD-CSF]]), bearings and output flange, as used in collaborative robots of the UR5e class. It is the main thread on the robotics side: the hollow joint shaft of Chapter 33 and the joint module of Chapter 45 both use it as the example, and Chapter 51 turns it into an integrated project.

### 1.9.4 How to use this book

Every chapter has the same structure: the problem, principles, worked examples, programs, virtual labs, engineering practice, engineer's notes and exercises. A few suggestions:

- Every number in the worked examples is computed by a program. Open the "Program" box to see the code; changing a parameter and running it again to see how the result changes is the quickest way to understand a formula.
- The virtual labs run in the browser and have lab instructions and report templates; they can be done in class or outside it.
- The engineering task sheets are carried out in the digital factory: like an engineer, you take on a task, hand in calculation sheets and models, go through review, and watch your design being machined and inspected. Task sheet TS-1-1 of this chapter is the first.
- Parts-library links: the standard parts mentioned in the text can be opened to look up their dimensions and 3D models.

**Engineer's notes**

- Machine design is an "integrating" subject: every chapter draws on the chapters before it. When you meet an unfamiliar concept, follow the cross-references in the book back to where it was introduced.
- Do not just do exercises; do projects. One small project carried right through requirements, concept, design, verification and manufacture teaches design better than a hundred exercises.

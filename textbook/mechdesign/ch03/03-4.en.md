### 3.4.1 The problem

The pillow block of the palletizing conveyor roller (Section 3.1) is to be handed to the foundry and the machine shop. The foundry needs to know the outer shape and which surfaces are not machined. The machine shop needs to know how large the bearing bore is to be, how much it may deviate, how smooth its surface must be, and how accurate the center height must be: if the center heights of the two pillow blocks at the ends of the same roller differ a little, the roller is skewed. The inspector needs to know the criteria for acceptance. The warehouse and the production planner need to know what the part is called and what it is made of. All of this must be written on the **part drawing**.

### 3.4.2 Content of a part drawing

A part drawing has four parts (Figure 3.4.1):

1. **A set of views**: these show the shape of the part completely and clearly. The pillow block is symmetrical about its center line, so the front view is a half section, showing the exterior and the anchor-bolt holes together. The left view is a full section (A–A) showing the steps of the bearing bore and the shoulder bore. The top view shows the positions of the base, the anchor-bolt holes and the oil hole.
2. **All dimensions**: every dimension needed for manufacture and inspection, given according to the principles of Section 3.3. The center height of {{ex3_4.H}} mm is a functional dimension; it is given directly from the mounting face, with its own tolerance of ±0.05. The anchor-bolt hole spacing of {{ex3_4.Lh:.0f}} mm is given directly and corresponds to the holes in the frame. The H7 after the bearing bore Ø{{ex3_4.D}}{{ex3_4.bore_fit}} is a **tolerance class**: H is the fundamental deviation (for a hole) and 7 the tolerance grade; together they set the upper and lower limits allowed for the hole, {{ex3_4.bore_lo:.3f}}–{{ex3_4.bore_hi:.3f}} mm. When a hole and a shaft are assembled, their tolerance classes are written as a fraction (such as Ø40 H7/r6), called a **fit designation** (Section 3.5). The meaning and choice of these designations are covered in Chapter 4; here it is enough to know how to write them.
3. **Technical requirements**: geometrical tolerances given by symbols (a parallelism tolerance of 0.03 mm for the axis of the bearing bore relative to datum A, the base), surface roughness, and requirements written in words: quality of the casting, aging treatment, unspecified casting radii, the standard for unspecified tolerances, and so on.
4. **Title block**: name, drawing number (item code), material, scale, quantity, designer and checker, date, revision, organization.

**Example 3.4.1** Program 3.4.1 generates the part drawing of the pillow block from the 3D model: A3 landscape, scale {{ex3_4.scale}}. All three views are obtained by projecting or sectioning the model, and the hatching is computed from the sections of the model. Only four places are machined: the bearing bore (Ra 1.6), the front and rear end faces (Ra 3.2) and the base (Ra 6.3, which is also datum A). The rest are cast surfaces, indicated collectively near the title block by the symbol for "material removal not permitted" (a small circle inside the V). The material is gray cast iron HT200; each roller uses two blocks, so the quantity in the title block is 2. The drawing number PB-6207 is a number used for the examples in this book.

### 3.4.3 Sheet sizes, scales, lines and lettering

- Sheet sizes (GB/T 14689): A0 to A4; A4 is 210 × 297 mm, and each size has twice the area of the next smaller one. The border is a thick continuous line; when the drawing is to be bound, 25 mm is left on the left.
- Scales (GB/T 14690): 1:1 is preferred; reduction scales are 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, ..., and enlargement scales 2:1, 5:1, 10:1, .... Whatever the scale, the dimensions written on the drawing are the true dimensions of the part.
- Lines (GB/T 4457.4): visible outlines are thick continuous lines; dimension lines and hatching are thin continuous lines; hidden outlines are thin dashed lines; axes and center lines of symmetry are thin chain lines. The thick line width d is 0.5–0.7 mm, and thin lines are about d/2.
- Lettering (GB/T 14691): Chinese characters are written in the elongated FangSong style, at least 3.5 mm high; figures and letters may be sloping or upright; the character height is chosen from 1.8, 2.5, 3.5, 5, 7, ....

The title block in the figures of this chapter is a simplified format commonly used for drawing exercises in schools, with content according to GB/T 10609.1; industrial title blocks have more fields (zones, a revision record, signature boxes and so on).

### 3.4.4 Indicating tolerances and roughness

- A size tolerance is written after the nominal size: the tolerance class only (Ø72H7), the limit deviations only (Ø72 +0.030/0), or both.
- A geometrical tolerance is given in a frame: the first compartment holds the characteristic symbol (such as ∥ for parallelism or ↗ for circular run-out), the second the tolerance value, and the third the datum letter. The arrow points to the toleranced feature: pointing to a surface controls that surface; aligned with a dimension line, it controls the axis of that segment. A datum is shown by a filled (or open) triangle and a letter in a square; the triangle is placed on the datum surface, or aligned with a dimension line to show that the datum is an axis.
- Surface roughness (GB/T 131-2006) uses the √-shaped symbol: a bar closing the top of the V means material removal is required (a machined surface), a small circle inside the V means material removal is not permitted (a cast or forged surface), and the parameter and value (such as Ra 1.6) are written above the long leg. When most surfaces are the same, this symbol is placed near the title block followed by a basic symbol in parentheses, meaning "all surfaces except those indicated on the drawing". Values of Ra are preferably taken from the series 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.3, 12.5 (GB/T 1031). The "▽" surface-finish symbols of the old standard have been withdrawn and should no longer be used.

### 3.4.5 Self-check list for part drawings

Before handing in a drawing, check the following eight items one by one (the self-check table of task sheet TS-3-1 is this list):

1. **Views**: the representation is complete with no redundant views; the direction of the front view is sensible;
2. **Sections**: cutting positions are sensible; hatching direction and spacing are consistent; the required cutting symbols, arrows and letters are all present;
3. **Lines**: line types and widths conform to GB/T 4457.4; center lines are complete; no lines are drawn at tangent edges;
4. **Dimensions**: datums are sensible; functional dimensions are given directly; no closed chains, no duplicates, nothing missing; no dimension value is crossed by any line;
5. **Tolerances**: tolerance classes agree with the mating parts; geometrical tolerances have datums; unspecified tolerances are stated in the technical requirements;
6. **Roughness**: every surface is covered (indicated individually or under "all other surfaces"); values are from the preferred series;
7. **Technical requirements**: material, heat treatment (or aging), hardness, unspecified radii and chamfers, and inspection requirements are clearly stated;
8. **Title block**: name, drawing number, material, scale, revision and signatures are all present.

The AI design reviewer of the digital factory reads the text from the submitted PDF and checks the items that can be checked automatically: whether the scale is a standard scale, whether Ra is a preferred value, whether a capital-letter (hole) tolerance class has been used by mistake on an external diameter of a shaft, whether there are obsolete symbols, whether the title block has a drawing number and a material grade, and whether every diameter of the 3D model appears on the drawing. The rest is judged by the teacher from the drawing.

**Engineer's notes**

- A part drawing is a contract: the shop floor makes the part to it and inspection judges the part by it. A requirement not on the drawing is no requirement at all.
- For every dimension and tolerance on a drawing you should be able to answer "why this value?". The ones you cannot account for have often been copied from some other drawing.
- On a casting drawing, distinguish machined from unmachined surfaces: dimensions between machined surfaces, and between machined surfaces and datums, must be accurate; unmachined surfaces follow the casting tolerances.
- When a drawing has been changed, its revision must be raised and the old drawing recalled or voided; otherwise the workshop will machine to the old drawing (Section 3.10).

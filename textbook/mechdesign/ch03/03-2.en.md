### 3.2.1 The problem

The hollow output shaft RJ-201 of a collaborative-robot shoulder joint (Section 33.10) has the reducer flange at one end, and at the other end a spline for the upper-arm flange and a thread for the nut that clamps the bearings. A bore of Ø{{ex3_2.rj_bore:.0f}} runs right through it so that cables can pass through the middle of the joint. If only the outside is drawn, the bore and the external steps all become dashed lines lying on top of one another, and nobody can make them out. How can the internal structure be shown clearly?

### 3.2.2 Kinds of view

Besides the three views, the national standards (GB/T 17451, GB/T 4458.1) provide several other kinds of view, to be used as needed:

- **Principal views**: the six views obtained by projecting from the front, back, top, bottom, left and right; they are placed in prescribed positions and need no labels;
- **Reference-arrow views**: principal views that may be placed freely; a capital letter (such as "B") is written above the view, and an arrow near the relevant view shows the direction of viewing;
- **Partial views**: principal views that show only part of the object, broken off from the rest by a wavy line or a zigzag line;
- **Auxiliary views**: where the object has an inclined feature, it is projected onto a plane parallel to that feature, so that its true shape is shown.

The number of views is set by what is needed to make the shape clear. Every additional view is one more place where an error can occur and one more thing to check.

### 3.2.3 Sectional views

A **sectional view** imagines the object cut by a cutting plane, removes the part between the observer and the cutting plane, and projects the rest onto the plane of projection. Where the cutting plane meets the object (the sectioned area), hatching is drawn: for metals, thin continuous lines at 45° to the horizontal. The hatching of one part must have the same direction and spacing in every view, and adjacent parts are hatched in opposite directions or with different spacing (Section 3.5). Once the object is cut open, most of the former dashed lines become continuous lines and the internal structure is plain to see. There are three kinds, according to how much is cut open (Figure 3.2.1):

- **Full section**: the object is cut right through by one cutting plane; used for parts with a simple exterior and a complicated interior. The exterior of RJ-201 is a few cylinders and its interior a through bore, so the front view is a full section (Figure 3.2.1a), with the cutting plane through the axis.
- **Half section**: for a symmetrical object, one half is drawn in outside view and the other half in section, the boundary being the center line of symmetry. The pillow block is symmetrical about its center line and both its exterior and interior must be shown, so a half section is used (Figure 3.2.1b); dashed lines are no longer drawn in the outside half.
- **Local (broken-out) section**: only a small part is cut open, separated from the uncut part by a wavy line. Whether the anchor-bolt holes of the pillow block are through holes or blind holes is clear at a glance from a local section in the front view (Figure 3.2.1c).

The cutting surface need not be a single plane. Several parallel planes (an offset section) or intersecting planes (an aligned section) may be used to bring features that do not lie in one plane into a single view. Holes equally spaced on a flange may be "rotated" into the cutting plane and drawn there, as the national standard permits, even if none of them lies in the cutting plane.

### 3.2.4 Cross-sections

A **cross-section** shows only the part of the object in contact with the cutting plane and nothing that lies behind it. Keyways, holes and flats on a shaft are best shown by cross-sections. The front view is drawn with the keyway facing the viewer, which shows the length and position of the keyway. The depth of the keyway cannot be seen in the front view, but a **removed section** drawn at the keyway (outside the view, with its outline as a thick continuous line) allows the depth and width of the keyway to be dimensioned (Figure 3.2.2). On SH-301 version B the keyway at the gear seat is {{ex3_2.kg_L:.0f}} mm long and the keyway on the overhang is {{ex3_2.kc_L:.0f}} mm long and runs out at the shaft end (an open keyway). The two sections A–A and B–B are drawn below their cutting positions.

**Engineer's notes**

- Choosing a section: a full section for a simple exterior and complicated interior; a half section for a symmetrical part whose interior and exterior must both be shown; a local section when only one internal feature needs to be seen.
- Solid parts such as solid shafts, keys, pins and bolts are drawn unsectioned (outline only) when cut lengthwise; local features on a shaft are shown by local sections or cross-sections.
- Hatching should not be parallel or perpendicular to the main outlines; when 45° clashes with an outline, 30° or 60° may be used instead.

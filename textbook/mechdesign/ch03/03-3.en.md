### 3.3.1 The problem

Along its axis, SH-301 version B has {{ex3_3.n_faces}} places whose positions must be fixed: two end faces, five shoulder faces, and the boundary on the Ø35 segment between the two tolerance classes k6 and f9. Just {{ex3_3.n_dims}} dimensions fix them completely. How should these {{ex3_3.n_dims}} dimensions be chosen? One after another in a chain, all from the left end face (baseline), or picked according to the function of the part? It looks like a mere matter of presentation, but every dimension carries a manufacturing error, and the errors are "passed along" from dimension to dimension. A different method of dimensioning secures a different accuracy for the few dimensions that really matter.

### 3.3.2 Dimension datums

Dimensioning begins with the choice of a **datum**, the origin from which dimensions are measured. By purpose there are three kinds:

- **Design datum**: a surface or line that determines the position of the part in the machine, such as the axis of a shaft or the locating shoulder of a bearing;
- **Process datum**: a surface used for locating and measuring during machining, such as the end face in turning or the center holes at both ends;
- **Inspection datum**: the origin of measurement during inspection.

It is best if the three coincide. When they cannot, important dimensions are given from the design datum to secure the function, and the remaining dimensions are arranged for convenience of machining and measurement.

### 3.3.3 Three methods of dimensioning and the accumulation of error

**Example 3.3.1** The axial dimensions of SH-301 version B. First, which dimensions matter?

1. The distance from the gear locating face (the left side of the collar) to the shoulder of the right bearing. It determines the axial position of the gear relative to the bearings and affects the alignment of the two meshing gears.
2. The length of the gear seat. It must be {{ex3_3.margin_hub:.0f}} mm shorter than the gear hub so that the sleeve can press against the hub.
3. The length of the overhang. It must be {{ex3_3.margin_cpl:.0f}} mm shorter than the coupling hub so that the shaft-end retaining plate can press against the coupling.
4. The overall length.

Assume that no dimension carries an individual tolerance and that the part is made to the general tolerances of GB/T 1804-m (medium class): ±{{ex3_3.tol_25:.1f}} mm for sizes of 6–30 mm, ±{{ex3_3.tol_53:.1f}} mm for 30–120 mm and ±{{ex3_3.tol_240:.1f}} mm for 120–400 mm. For the three methods of dimensioning (Figure 3.3.1), the limit deviations of the four functional dimensions accumulate by the worst-case method as follows:

- **Chain dimensioning**: each segment length is given separately. The distance from the gear locating face to the right bearing shoulder passes through two dimensions, accumulating ±{{ex3_3.gear_chain:.1f}} mm. The overall length is not given directly and passes through all {{ex3_3.n_chain_tot}} dimensions, accumulating ±{{ex3_3.tot_chain:.1f}} mm (still ±{{ex3_3.tot_chain_rss:.2f}} mm by the statistical method).
- **Baseline dimensioning**: everything is measured from the left end face. The overall length is given directly (±{{ex3_3.tot_base:.1f}}), but the distance from the gear locating face to the right bearing shoulder is the difference of two large dimensions, accumulating ±{{ex3_3.gear_base:.1f}} mm. The length of the overhang is the difference between 172 and 240, accumulating ±{{ex3_3.ext_base:.1f}} mm, which in the worst case uses up half of the 2 mm margin left for the coupling hub.
- **Combined dimensioning (by function)**: the functional dimensions are given directly: gear locating face to right bearing shoulder ±{{ex3_3.gear_func:.1f}}, gear seat length ±{{ex3_3.seat_func:.1f}}, overhang length ±{{ex3_3.ext_func:.1f}}, overall length ±{{ex3_3.tot_func:.1f}}. The error accumulates in dimensions that do not matter (the lengths of the Ø35 segment at the left end and of the seal seat are not given).

The conclusion is clear. The dimensions that matter are given directly, so that each is affected by one dimensional tolerance only; the dimensions that do not matter are left as the "open" link, and the error accumulates in them. This is what "dimensioning by function" means. This chain of relations between dimensions is called a **dimension chain**, and Chapter 4 uses it to calculate tolerances.

### 3.3.4 Some rules of dimensioning

- **No closed dimension chains**: when a string of dimensions joined end to end is completed by the overall dimension, the chain is closed. Every dimension has a tolerance, so they cannot all be satisfied at once, and the workshop does not know which one to hold. Of the overall length and the segment lengths, only n − 1 may be given.
- **No duplicate dimensions**: each dimension is given once, in the view that shows it best.
- **Consider machining and measurement**: dimensions should be given from surfaces that can be measured directly. For example, the depth of a keyway in a shaft is given as "shaft diameter minus keyway depth" (d − t, where t is the depth of the shaft keyway), which can be measured directly with a caliper from the bottom of the keyway to the opposite cylindrical surface.
- **Values and presentation to GB/T 4458.4**: dimension values are written above the dimension line (for vertical dimensions, on the left of the line, with the tops of the figures facing left, so that they read from the right-hand side of the drawing); a diameter is preceded by Ø and a radius by R; dimension lines are thin continuous lines with arrowheads at both ends.

**Engineer's notes**

- Before dimensioning, think about what the part mates with in the machine and what locates it. Dimensioning in the order of the features of the 3D model tends to produce chain dimensioning.
- General tolerances (GB/T 1804) are stated in the technical requirements, and all dimensions without an individual tolerance are inspected to them; dimensions that matter are toleranced individually.
- To check the dimensions of a drawing: the position of every face can be reached from the drawing by exactly one path of dimensions, with nothing redundant and nothing missing.

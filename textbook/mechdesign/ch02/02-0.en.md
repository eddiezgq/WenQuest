title: "Chapter Overview"
---

Chapter 1 said that a design passes through seven stages: requirements, concept, detail design, verification, release, manufacture and service. This chapter is mainly about the methods engineers use in the first two stages, requirements and concept, together with the methods of systems engineering and standardization that run through the whole process. These two stages account for little of the spending, yet they determine most of the cost of the product: once the concept has been chosen, no amount of careful economy in detail design can change the big decisions, such as "gears or a belt" and "one stage or two".

The chapter follows the order in which an engineer develops a concept. Section 2.1 turns what the customer says into a checkable design specification and uses the house of quality to find the engineering characteristics that matter most. Section 2.2 decomposes the overall function into subfunctions, lists several solutions for each subfunction and arranges them in a morphological matrix. Section 2.3 deals with how to come up with more and better solutions. Section 2.4 compares concepts with the Pugh matrix and weighted scoring, and checks whether the conclusion is robust to the weights. Section 2.5 covers the V-model of systems engineering and stage-gates. Section 2.6 covers standardization, serialization, modularization and parametric product families. Section 2.7 discusses what AI can and cannot do in design, and how to check its output.

Two examples run through the chapter: the WQR-105 reducer (house of quality, V-model, product family) and a new design problem, the docking mechanism of an automatic charging dock for the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot (morphological matrix, concept evaluation). The task sheet of this chapter, TS-2-1, has readers work in groups to develop the concept for this docking mechanism and take it through review.

After studying this chapter the reader should be able to:

1. rewrite customer requirements as design specifications with values and test methods, draw a house of quality and compute the importance of the engineering characteristics;
2. draw the function structure of a product, list concepts systematically with a morphological matrix, and rule out incompatible combinations;
3. generate concepts by brainstorming, analogy and the inventive principles of TRIZ;
4. evaluate concepts with the Pugh matrix and weighted scoring, and check the sensitivity of the conclusion to the weights;
5. explain the correspondence between requirement decomposition and verification in the V-model, and the role of stage-gates;
6. design a product family with preferred numbers and parametric methods, and select a model with a configurator;
7. state what AI is suited to do in design, and the methods of checking its results.

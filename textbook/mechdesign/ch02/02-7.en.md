### 2.7.1 The problem

Today an engineer can hand a single sentence to an AI: "pick a coupling for me that can transmit 350 N·m", "how should the constraints be applied in the finite element model of this shaft?", "review this calculation sheet". The AI answers quickly and in a confident tone. Is what it says right? What can be entrusted to it and what cannot? If it is wrong, who is responsible?

### 2.7.2 What AI can do

In machine design, AI (here mainly large language models and the tools that work with them) can already help reliably with the following:

1. **Organizing and searching.** Distilling requirements from customers' emails and meeting notes (Section 2.1), searching standards, patents and existing designs, drafting documents.
2. **Turning natural language into structured settings.** The "one sentence" function of the digital factory's "Simulation & Analysis" turns "supported at two Ø35 bearing seats, rotation restrained at the right-hand end, 350 N·m of torque applied on the side of the keyway" into tables of constraints and loads, and lists which face it has matched each phrase to.
3. **Checking against rules.** The digital factory's AI design reviewer checks deliverables against the review points of the task sheet (whether the calculation sheet gives its sources, whether the steps of the model have fillets, whether the change order lists all the affected items) and gives "must fix" and "suggestion" comments.
4. **Proposing candidate solutions.** Adding solutions to the morphological matrix (Section 2.2), proposing the next set of parameters in an optimization (Chapter 39).
5. **Explaining results.** Explaining a stress contour plot in plain words and pointing out the most dangerous location and its likely cause.

### 2.7.3 What AI cannot do

1. **It cannot be responsible for the results.** It is the engineer who signs (Section 1.8). Every number an AI gives must be checked by a person before it goes into a drawing or a calculation sheet.
2. **It cannot guarantee that numbers are right.** A language model will "invent" plausible-looking values, standard numbers and sources. If it says "the basic dynamic load rating of the 6207 is 25.5 kN", check it in the parts library or the catalog; if it says "according to Table 3 of GB/T 1096", open the standard and see whether that table really exists.
3. **It does not know what you have not told it.** The duty, the customer's special requirements, the equipment of the factory, past failure experience: the AI knows none of these unless they were written into the prompt or the data.
4. **It cannot replace judgment.** In the example of Section 2.4, whether A or C is better depends on what the customer values more, and that question can only be taken back to the customer.

### 2.7.4 How to check

Checking the results of an AI is in essence no different from checking the work of a new colleague:

- **Estimate the order of magnitude.** Make a rough estimate with Eq. (1.1.2) or a simple beam formula; if the result is out by an order of magnitude, something is certainly wrong (the unit errors of Section 1.6).
- **Calculate again by another method.** Check stresses that an AI computes with finite elements against the hand-calculated nominal stress times the stress concentration factor (Chapter 33).
- **Look up the sources.** Every standard value and every item of material data must be traceable to a standard, a catalog or the parts library.
- **See what it has "understood".** The "one sentence" function of the digital factory lists which face and which load it has matched each phrase to, and flags what it did not understand; check this matching table first, then look at the results.
- **Let the AI do only what it is good at.** The digital factory's AI reviewer only checks against rules and gives comments, and the authority to approve rests with the teacher; deterministic calculations such as the configurator and the finite element solver are done by programs, and the AI is responsible only for translating words into settings and results into words.

Systems deployed in China use only models that have been registered with the authorities; this too is one of the regulatory constraints to be observed when choosing AI for engineering software.

---

**Engineer's notes**

- Treat AI as a capable but inexperienced assistant that occasionally makes mistakes with great confidence: give it organizing, drafting and checklist-type work, and keep judgment and signing for yourself.
- Make your prompts to an AI as specific as a design specification: duty, units, standards, and the output format you want.
- The AI's answers are part of the engineering record too: wherever AI was used, state in the calculation sheet what was used and how it was checked.

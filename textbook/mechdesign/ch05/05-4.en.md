### 5.4.1 Aluminum alloys

The density of aluminum alloys is only a little more than a third of that of steel, and their elastic modulus is also about a third of steel's, so the specific stiffness E/ρ is almost the same as steel's. Their strengths are light weight, ease of manufacture (fast machining, extrusion of complex sections, die casting), resistance to atmospheric corrosion and good thermal conductivity. Robot links, joint housings and bases, and the profile frames of automation equipment, make heavy use of aluminum alloys.

In the designation (the designation system is in GB/T 16474, the compositions in GB/T 3190; most designations agree with the international four-digit system) the first digit gives the main alloying element: 6xxx is the magnesium–silicon series (6061, 6063: medium strength, easy to extrude, corrosion-resistant; profiles and general structural parts); 7xxx is the zinc–magnesium–copper series (7075: the highest strength, for aircraft and highly loaded structural parts, with poorer corrosion resistance and weldability); 5xxx is the magnesium series (non-heat-treatable, resistant to seawater); 2xxx is the copper series (for example the Chinese 2A12, close to 2024: high strength, poor corrosion resistance; 2A12 is a Chinese designation with a letter and is not part of the international four-digit system). The **temper designation** that follows (GB/T 16475) matters a great deal: T6 means solution heat-treated and then artificially aged, giving high strength; O is the annealed condition, with less than half the strength. For the same 6061, the yield strength in the T6 temper is several times that in the O temper, so the temper must be stated on the drawing.

Aluminum alloys have no true fatigue limit (Section 5.1.4), so for links and brackets under alternating load the fatigue strength at a specified life is used.

### 5.4.2 Titanium and magnesium alloys

- **Titanium alloys.** The most widely used is TC4 (Ti-6Al-4V): E = {{ex5_7.E_ti:.0f}} GPa, ρ = {{ex5_7.rho_ti:.2f}} g/cm³, yield strength about {{ex5_7.sy_ti:.0f}} MPa (typical values from the table of this book). High specific strength, corrosion resistance and biocompatibility make them suitable for lightweight load-bearing parts in aircraft, medical devices and high-end robots. They are expensive and difficult to machine (poor thermal conductivity, a tendency to stick to the tool), but well suited to additive manufacturing (Section 5.6).
- **Magnesium alloys.** The lightest structural metal: AZ31B has ρ = {{ex5_7.rho_mg:.2f}} g/cm³ and E = {{ex5_7.E_mg:.0f}} GPa. Its specific stiffness is still about the same as that of steel and aluminum, but for the same stiffness the wall can be thicker, so thin-walled parts are less prone to buckling (Section 5.7). Corrosion resistance is poor and surface treatment is needed; the chips are flammable, so machining calls for care. Wrought magnesium alloys (such as AZ31B) are used for sheet and profiles; die castings commonly use AZ91D or AM60B and are found in laptop housings, drones and some robot arms.

### 5.4.3 Copper alloys

Copper alloys conduct electricity and heat well and have good wear resistance and low friction. Brass (copper–zinc) machines well and is used for fittings and valve bodies; tin bronze and aluminum bronze resist wear and are used for plain bearings and worm-wheel rims (Chapter 30): the worm is hardened steel and the wheel bronze, the two forming a low-friction pair. Beryllium copper (beryllium bronze) has good elasticity and is used for conductive springs. Copper is much more expensive than steel and is used only where its special properties are needed.

**Engineer's notes**

- "Aluminum is lighter than steel" does not mean "an aluminum part is lighter than a steel part": for the same stiffness, if the dimensions cannot be increased, the aluminum part is no lighter (Section 5.7). The benefit of aluminum is that the section can be made larger and the walls thicker without adding weight.
- Aluminum in contact with steel suffers galvanic corrosion, so outdoors and in damp environments the two must be isolated (washers, coatings). The coefficient of thermal expansion of aluminum is twice that of steel, so fits must be checked for temperature (Section 4.3.5).
- The prices, supply and machining conditions of titanium and magnesium alloys vary widely; consult the factory and the suppliers before choosing them.

### 5.8.1 Why cross-reference?

The supply chain of robots and automation equipment is global: the motors, bearings and reducers in the parts library come from manufacturers in many countries, and drawings may specify materials by American, European or Japanese standards, while Chinese factories purchase to GB standards. Reading foreign drawings, issuing drawings to foreign customers and finding substitute materials all require looking up the correspondence between **material designations**.

The naming schemes of different countries differ:

- **China, GB**: carbon steels by carbon content (45), alloy steels by carbon content plus elements (40Cr), structural steels by yield strength (Q235), cast irons by tensile strength (HT200);
- **United States**: AISI/SAE four-digit numbers denote the type of steel (10xx carbon steels, 41xx chromium–molybdenum steels, 51xx chromium steels, the last two digits being the carbon content in hundredths of a percent); an ASTM standard number with a grade denotes structural steels and cast irons (A36, A48 Class 30);
- **Europe, EN**: a name based on application or composition (S235JR structural steel, C45 carbon steel, 41Cr4 alloy steel) plus a numerical material number;
- **Japan, JIS**: letters and digits (S45C, SCr440, SS400, FC200).

### 5.8.2 Approximate correspondence of common designations

The table below reproduces the digital table `std/grade_xref.yaml` of this book (compiled from the cross-reference tables of various handbooks, marked "to be verified"):

| GB | ASTM / AISI / SAE | EN | JIS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q235B | ASTM A36 (approx.) | S235JR | SS400 |
| Q355B | ASTM A572 Gr.50 (approx.) | S355JR | SM490A (approx.) |
| 45 | AISI/SAE 1045 | C45 | S45C |
| 40Cr | AISI/SAE 5140 | 41Cr4 | SCr440 |
| 20CrMnTi | no direct equivalent (used like SAE 8620) | no direct equivalent | no direct equivalent |
| HT200 | ASTM A48 Class 30 | EN-GJL-200 | FC200 |
| QT500-7 | ASTM A536 80-55-06 (approx., somewhat stronger) | EN-GJS-500-7 | FCD500-7 |
| 06Cr19Ni10 (304) | 304 | X5CrNi18-10 | SUS304 |
| 6061 | 6061 | EN AW-6061 | A6061 |
| 7075 | 7075 | EN AW-7075 | A7075 |

Correspondence does not mean equivalence. The composition ranges, delivery condition, test-piece size and impact test temperature may all differ. For example, Q235B and A36 have different lower limits of yield strength; 45 steel contains 0.42%–0.50% carbon and 0.50%–0.80% manganese, while the Japanese S45C has 0.42%–0.48% and 0.60%–0.90%; 20CrMnTi is a case-hardening steel unique to China, with no directly corresponding designation abroad. When using a substitute material, check the composition and properties of the other standard item by item, run verification tests if necessary, and record it in an engineering change order (Section 3.10).

---

**Engineer's notes**

- When a drawing gives a foreign designation, state the standard number with it (for example "C45, EN 10083-2"), not just a code.
- Before buying a substitute material, ask three things: is the composition within range, on what test pieces were the mechanical properties measured, and what is the delivery condition?
- In the item codes of the digital factory, raw materials are coded by designation and size (for example RM-45-D50), so changing the designation means changing the code (Section 3.10).

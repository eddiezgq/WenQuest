### 33.9.1 The problem

In the previous eight sections we worked out version B of SH-301 by hand calculation, standards look-up and programs. In a factory, an engineer does not hand in a calculation sheet alone: he selects the bearings, keys and oil seal from the parts library, builds a three-dimensional model in CAD and produces the part drawing, checks strength, stiffness and fatigue life with CAE software, and submits the design for review; after approval the ERP system generates a new design revision, and only then does the workshop machine and inspect to the new drawing. This section maps every calculation step of this chapter onto the pages of the WenQuest digital factory and walks through them in the order an engineer actually follows. The reader carries it out in task sheet TS-33-1 and factory Lab 12 (Lab 12 also uses the two case-study shafts of Sections 33.10 and 33.11).

### 33.9.3 Step 1: Select the standard parts from the parts library

All the standard parts on SH-301 are in the WenQuest parts library: two deep-groove ball bearings [[part:A-BRG-DG/6207]], the parallel keys [[part:A-KEY-FLAT]] at the gear seat and the coupling seat (sizes {{ex33_9.key_gear}} and {{ex33_9.key_cpl}}, Section 33.2), and the lip seal [[part:A-SEL-LIP]] on the sealing segment. Following these links opens the corresponding pages in the parts library: the dimension table, the standard number and the three-dimensional model (a STEP file can be downloaded, and FreeCAD users can insert it directly into an assembly with the macro `wq_library.py`).

Selecting from the parts library, instead of "drawing a bearing" oneself, has three advantages: the dimensions come from the standard and cannot be copied wrongly; the same part number means the same thing in the BOM, in purchasing and in the warehouse; and at assembly one can check directly whether the bearing interferes with the shoulder or the housing. The fillet radius of Section 33.2.5 must be fixed according to the inner-ring chamfer $r_\mathrm s$ given in the parts library; the shoulder height also depends on the mounting dimension $d_\mathrm a$, for which the parts library has no column yet, so look it up in GB/T 5868 or in the bearing manufacturer's catalogue.

### 33.9.4 Step 2: Modelling and drawing

SH-301 is a typical part of revolution, and modelling it means "stack cylinders along the axis, then add fillets and cut the keyways". The digital factory offers two ways:

- The web design desk (the "Design & Process" page, no software to install): enter the diameter and length of each segment and the keyways, and enter the "Step fillet" (the transition fillet at the inner corner of each step; a shoulder against which a bearing sits must have a fillet smaller than the bearing's inner-ring chamfer) → view the 3D model and the checks → "Publish new revision". The server generates the STEP file, the part drawing and the NC program for the keyway.
- A parametric script: Program 33.9.1 uses build123d (Chapter 3) to build version B from the table of Section 33.2: seven cylindrical segments, {{ex33_9.n_fillets}} step fillets (r = {{ex33_9.r_min:.1f}}–{{ex33_9.r_max:.1f}} mm), and two type-A keyways cut along the same line of the surface (Figure 33.9.2). The exported STEP file is uploaded in "Design Release & Approval". Any CAD system (FreeCAD, SolidWorks, ZWCAD) can be used in this way.

Once the model is built, check it yourself first: the volume is {{ex33_9.vol_cm3:.1f}} cm³ and the mass in 45 steel is {{ex33_9.mass:.2f}} kg; the fillets and keyways together reduce the volume by {{ex33_9.dvol:.1f}} cm³. If the mass differs much from the hand calculation, some segment dimension has been entered wrongly. The advantage of a parametric script is that changing one number rebuilds the model: the two case-study shafts of Sections 33.10 and 33.11 are built in the same way.

### 33.9.5 Steps 3–5: Strength, stiffness, natural frequency and fatigue life in "Simulation & Analysis"

"Simulation & Analysis" is the CAE software of the digital factory (Gmsh meshes with second-order tetrahedra, CalculiX solves, pyLife computes fatigue); the programs of Sections 33.5 and 33.8 of this chapter call its code. For SH-301 version B:

- **Operation (1) Load the part.** Choose the part SH-301 (the newly published revision), or upload a STEP file. Rotate the model and move the mouse over the faces to identify the two bearing seats, the gear seat, the side faces of the keyway and the overhanging segment.
- **Operation (2) Material.** 45 steel (quenched and tempered); for comparing schemes, change to 40Cr (quenched and tempered).
- **Operation (3) Constraints.** Click "+ Bearing support" on the left bearing seat (tick "Thrust"), then on the right bearing seat; click "+ Restrain rotation" on the overhanging segment. The principle of constraints is in Section 33.8: remove only the rigid-body motion, and do not clamp the cross-section.
- **Operation (4) Loads.** Apply the gear forces (the tangential and radial forces found in Section 33.4) on the gear seat, and the torque on the side face of the keyway or on the gear seat. One may also write the setup in Chinese in the "One sentence" box and let "AI fill" complete the form, then check item by item whether the faces it chose are right.
- **Operation (5) Mesh and solve.** First solve with "Medium", then again with "Fine" (for a slender shaft or one of small diameter one may also enter a size directly in "or set element size", for example 3 mm), and compare whether the stress in the smooth segments is stable (Section 33.8).
- **Operation (6) Check.** Compare the stress in the smooth segments with the hand-calculated $\sigma = M/W$ and $\tau = T/W_\mathrm T$; they should agree within a few percent. The ratio of the peak stress at a step fillet to the nominal stress should be close to $\alpha_\sigma$ of Section 33.5. A finite-element result that has not been checked must not be used.
- **Operation (7) Stiffness.** Read the displacements; compare the deflection at the gear and the slope at the bearings with Section 33.6.
- **Operation (8) Natural frequencies.** "Compute natural frequencies" gives the first few natural frequencies and the corresponding speeds. For this step, tick "Middle ring only (pinned)" in the bearing support: a deep-groove ball bearing allows the shaft a small rotation and is close to a pinned support, while restraining the radial displacement over the whole bearing seat amounts to clamping the shaft and makes the natural frequencies much too high (Section 33.11.5 has a worked example). SH-301 is a low-speed shaft, so this step only confirms that the working speed is far from the natural frequencies; for a high-speed shaft it is indispensable.
- **Operation (9) Fatigue life.** Below the results, choose the load spectrum: constant amplitude, the torque record of the run-in test bench (Section 33.8), the "Joint-torque record" of "Multibody Dynamics" (the robot joint shaft of Section 33.10), or "Upload load record" with your own CSV (the upper shaft of the sewing machine in Section 33.11, used for torsion only); enter the surface finish and the size factor, compute the life, and read the "main cycles at the most dangerous point".
- **Operation (10) Report.** "Download report (Word)": the report contains the setup, the contour plots, the checks and the life, and goes with the calculation sheet as an annex.

If any one of operations (6)–(9) is not satisfied, go back to design step 2 and change the design (for example, thicken the overhanging segment or change to 40Cr), publish it again, and calculate once more. The "Try in the design desk" button of "Simulation & Analysis" carries the suggested dimensions to the design desk.

### 33.9.6 Steps 6–7: Review, release and manufacture

When the calculation sheet, model, drawings and report are complete, open task sheet TS-33-1 in "My tasks" of the digital factory and upload the deliverables one by one. Before submitting, one may click "Pre-check": the AI design reviewer checks the work against the review points of the task sheet (for example, whether the deliverables are complete and whether the calculation sheet lists all the items it should) and gives comments of two kinds, "must fix" and "suggestion". The teacher annotates item by item on the review page; the student replies, revises and submits again. Once the teacher approves, the new revision of the part enters "Design Release & Approval", and the design revision of SH-301 in ERPNext goes up by one.

Manufacture follows: when the work order is issued, the keyway operation is dispatched to the workshop together with the NC program of the new revision (factory Lab 10 treats NC programming); after machining, the coordinate measuring machine inspects against the dimensions and tolerances of the new drawing, and the results are written into the quality record. If the inspection finds a problem, an engineering change order is opened and we return to step 2. Design, manufacture and inspection form a closed loop in one system; this is what "digital factory" means.

**Engineer's notes**

- It is easy to get a beautiful contour plot from CAE software; the hard part is knowing whether it is right. Whenever you use a new model or a new way of constraining, first find a place that can be calculated by hand and check against it once.
- The dimensions in the model, the calculation sheet and the report must be of the same revision. The design revision number of the digital factory exists for this purpose: review comments and calculation reports record which revision they refer to.
- The standard-part number in the parts library is a "name" used throughout the factory. Write this number in the parts list of the drawing, in the BOM and in purchase orders, rather than a name of your own.
